Why Dueproof exists.

A notice is a clock that starts when it reaches you. In most offices it is read once, by one person, and its date lives in that person's head or a spreadsheet nobody trusts.

Dueproof started as a student project for document overload at a public transit operator: thousands of bilingual circulars, many arriving as phone photos, with deadlines buried in the middle of page five. What we learned there holds for anyone who receives official paper in India: the date matters more than the summary, the reader needs to see where it came from, and the documents cannot go to someone else's cloud.

So Dueproof does one job carefully. It finds what a document asks of you and by when, puts it on a register with an owner, and shows the sentence behind every entry.

What we will not build

  • A cloud service that holds your documents. Running on your machine is the point.
  • Something that acts on its own. No auto-filed replies, no auto-payments. It proposes; a person decides.
  • A model in place of the rules. Being able to say why a sentence was flagged is the product.
  • Confident guesses. If a passage cannot be located, Dueproof says so.
